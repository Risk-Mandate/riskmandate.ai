The questions

What a sceptical engineer asks, and the honest answer.

Are you inline now?
No. SHarness runs where the customer runs it, compiles once at launch, and costs nothing per request. RiskMandate sees no traffic and holds no key.
Why not just use a sandbox, Docker or a gateway?
Use them: SHarness is a reference implementation, and any enforcer that holds the same rows is valid. policy translate writes the policy for bubblewrap, Docker, Kubernetes and Claude Code's sandbox, and lists what each cannot hold. The translations are written, not yet run against those targets.
What stops the agent rewriting its own policy?
The hash. The person supplies sha256 from outside and the enforcer refuses anything else; the enforcer is something the agent does not include. A policy a process applies to its own child is a boundary for the child and only a setting for the parent, and SHarness prints which it was.
False positives?
There is no measured rate: most of it is a design. The argument is a policy layered down to the business, and CI that fails when the footprint grows without a policy change.
Who reviewed the security?
So far, an agent of the project. Say so; ask him to try to break the shell.
Mac or Windows?
Not today: Linux only, and no protection against root.
Rehearse

What can go wrong on the day.

From the SHarness 0.2.0 user report and the status page, as at 29 October 2026.

  • Run hello from a virtual environment: after a per-user pip install it held 0 of 6 in the 0.2.0 user report; from a venv, 6 of 6.
  • The quick start typed word for word fails in 0.2.0; use the commands on sharness.ai/code/examples.
  • A binary the child process wrote ran through the dynamic loader, around the execute rule: known, reported, not fixed in 0.2.0.
  • A refusal prints the shell's Permission denied, not that the kernel refused: say what it means.
  • The browser kernel takes about two minutes to boot: start it first.
  • The film says delta where the site says gap: the same thing.
