What RiskMandate is. RiskMandate.ai helps organisations know what their AI agents can do. Its line is: "Know what your agents can do. Not what they did. What they can." The context: insurers are starting to exclude AI from cover, and businesses answer for what their agents do. RiskMandate is UK-based, early-stage and founder-led, and it is not an insurer.

The Agent Behaviour Policy, in more detail. Use this after my first reaction, not before. The Agent Behaviour Policy, or ABP, is written for one AI agent in one deployment, and it records four things: the mandate, which is what the business authorised the agent to do; the grant, which is everything the agent can actually do with the access it was given, read from the deployment itself or from the vendor's own published pages, with dates; the gap between the two; and, for each thing in the gap, what stands in the way, which is one of four kinds: nothing at all, an instruction the agent is expected to follow, a setting the agent's own account could switch off, or a boundary the agent cannot cross. The ABP never scores or rates the agent. It states what the agent can do, on a date, with its sources. The site's worked example is Claude with the Gmail connector: the connector's own tile says it drafts replies, summarises threads and searches the inbox, while the consent screen grants "Read, compose and send emails from your Gmail account."
