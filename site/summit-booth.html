Four things go through the portal. Not by email.

The guide is explicit: “Please do not email your logo separately if you can upload it through the portal.” Everything below goes to startupsummit.io/exhibitor/portal, on the account attached to the booking.

  • ☐

    Upload the logo — and check the company name on the account

    They print it centrally on the front panel. Artwork is ready below. The name must read RiskMandate — one word, two capitals. Adriano Wagner asked for the logo and exact name by Tuesday; the guide says the portal is the route, so upload first, then reply to him saying it is in the portal rather than attaching it.

    Due Tue 15 Sept
  • ☐

    Request power

    “Power will not automatically be available at every single booth position.” The permission game is meant to run on a screen facing the aisle for two days, which needs continuous mains. They use this to plan booth positions, so late is the same as never.

    Before the floor plan closes
  • ☐

    Declare the screen, if it is bigger than a laptop

    A laptop or tablet needs no permission. Anything larger needs telling so they can check space and power. Decide which it is before asking, because the answer changes the power request too.

    Same message as power
  • ☐

    Decide on side-panel branding — and only then produce anything

    Approx. 1000mm high × 400mm deep per side. Allowed subject to approval, and it must be removable, professionally produced, leave no damage, and stay inside our footprint. “Tell us through your portal so our team can approve it before the event.” My view: skip it. A 40cm-deep side panel is nearly invisible in an aisle and it is the one item that could miss the print window.

    Before ordering print

Booth number, exhibitor access time and final setup instructions come later, by email and through the portal. Nothing to chase — “if you haven't received your booth number yet, you haven't missed anything.”

What this changes

The roll-up banner comes off the list.

Our 9 September plan had a pull-up banner as a P0 print item. The guide puts large roll-up banners on the check-with-us list twice, and spends a page arguing against exactly that instinct: “You really don't need to build another Web Summit stand.” Three consequences.

  • Drop the banner. It needs approval, it may not be granted, it would have to be ordered today to arrive, and the room is being designed to not have them. The printed draft policies and the cards are the print order.
  • 40cm of depth sets the format. A laptop plus a small card holder plus a QR stand fills the top. A one-pager stack does not fit and would not be read — the draft card replaces it, which is what the Lisbon brief already argued on other grounds.
  • Small tabletop signage is explicitly fine and needs no approval. That is where the aisle line goes: “Do you know what your AI agent can actually do?” — one small sign, no structure.

Ready to upload

The artwork, in the formats they asked for.

Preferred: SVG, AI, EPS or a print-quality PDF. A high-resolution transparent PNG is also accepted. Upload one file — the PDF is the one to send.

FileWhat it isUse
riskmandate-booth-panel.pdf 200 × 200mm vector artboard, lockup centred, glyph outlines embedded Send this one
riskmandate-booth-panel-2000.png 2000 × 2000px, transparent, lockup centred If they want a raster
riskmandate-lockup-light.svg The lockup as vector, editable If they ask for SVG
riskmandate-mark-512.png The mark alone, square, 512px Only if they want a square mark
  • Send the lockup, not the mark alone. The front panel is a square metre at waist height in a room of 200 stands. The mark is recognisable only to people who already know us; the wordmark is what makes a stranger able to ask for us by name.
  • The PDF is a square artboard on purpose. The panel is 1000 × 1000mm, so a square file centres predictably however they place it. If they want it to fill the width instead, they can scale it — it is vector.
  • Ink and green only, on whatever ground the MDF gives us. Never recoloured, never stretched, and the detailed cut never below 24px. The five things not to do.
  • One defect fixed on the way. The shipped lockup SVG had a 260-unit viewBox around a <text> wordmark, so it clipped its own last letter in any font wider than the one it was drawn against — which is what a printer would have seen. The viewBox is now 320, and the print deliverables are laid out as real type rather than as a fixed-width drawing, so there is nothing left to clip.
