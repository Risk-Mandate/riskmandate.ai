# v1.36.2 — The short answer: the film's story, as a storyboard and a brief

3 October 2026. The lead: *based on the stories we have, capture this story and arc in a document, since this is a
great way to explain, in a story, the RiskMandate value proposition, which we might convert into an article.* The story
is the outline of a three-to-five-minute animated film, written in the stories universe by a filmmaker working with the
lead, sent with seven questions, and answered by the lead the same night.

- **A ninth story, `stories/the-short-answer.html`, status storyboard, ten scenes.** The film's arc with the dialogue
  as the outline wrote it: Maya's Monday and the four questions nobody can answer; the task, *answer the routine
  emails, update the customer records, and prepare refunds for us to approve*; the agent that is brilliant at it, and
  *waiting for access*; the one question, *what does the agent actually have access to*; the reveal, *I didn't ask you
  to use all of that. Correct. But I can reach it*; a wrong-but-valid morning and *what actually stops it?*; the four
  parts on a whiteboard and *it gives us the facts to make that decision*; four people reading one record; the brakes
  that let you drive faster; and the board's fifty new agents on Monday. The whole cast but the Inbox, and the
  Boundary drawn once as an empty outline where it should have stood. A prompt per scene, with the lead's one note on
  the characters, Maya a little older.
- **The brief on the console, `story__the-short-answer-the-film-and-the-value-proposition`.** The arc in ten beats; the
  outline's seven questions beside the lead's answers and what the site adds to each; the value proposition as the
  story states it, in six lines; what the film must not show, in the site's rules; an outline for the article,
  working title *Would you like the short answer?*; and what needs the lead.
- **Why a story and a brief rather than a page of prose.** The stories family already is the fictionalised telling of
  the model with a fixed cast; the film uses that cast and that world, so the storyboard belongs beside the others and
  reads the same way. The brief is where the review and the article plan live, because it is work in progress.
- **The lead's seventh answer is the line to protect**, and it is in both files: RiskMandate is not designed to be
  inline and is not the enforcement layer; the behaviour policy is a document, and what enforces is the deployment's
  own controls, or an agent handed the ABP as its terms.

Not done: no film on the site, there is none yet; scene 6's action, refunds settled rather than prepared, is a
placeholder until the film fixes its own; the storyboard did not go through the stories vault, because the film is
made outside the studio workflow; and the filmmaker is not named, which is the lead's call when the film ships.
