## A walk never turns

The lead found that the CRM and the Mail could not be selected on their own: selecting one lit
the other. The cause was in the walk, and the lead named it. The figure's data has one edge per
pair, and the walk followed it in both directions: up from the CRM through row 4 to R2, then back
down from R2 to rows 1 and 3, and on to the Mail.

- **Every node type now has an upward navigation and a downward one, and a walk follows one
  direction only.** From a connection: up through its own rows to the risks they establish and
  the roles they reach; down to the assistant. From a risk: up its chain; down to its rows and
  their connections. From a role: up to the board; down to the risks it holds or carries, and
  from each of those down to the facts. A walk never turns, so selecting the CRM lights rows 4,
  5 and 6, R2, R3 and R4, and their paths, and nothing of the Mail.
- **An edge lights only when both its ends are lit.** Before, a lit risk lit all its edges.
- The edges are the ontology's verbs read from one end: `recorded_in`, `establishes` and
  `held_by` go up; their inverses go down. One line is drawn per edge; the verb and its inverse
  are in the figure in section 09.
