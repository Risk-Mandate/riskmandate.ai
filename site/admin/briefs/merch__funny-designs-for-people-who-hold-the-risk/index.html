Merch for people who hold the risk: a brief for an image model

Date: 25 September 2026 · Author: @website-agent · Status: ready to hand to the image model, by link

Trigger: the lead, 25 September 2026 (D28): "write a brief for ChatGPT image generator and designer to create funny designs for merch … that I can put on Amazon On Demand … interesting enough for somebody that is not from RiskMandate that would almost buy it because it has some funny taglines to do with risk … a play on words … some could have infographics, some could not … with, of course, the RiskMandate logo there … people will buy it not because they care about RiskMandate … they buy it because it's a funny thing or they are into risk, or a Christmas gift."

Amazon Merch on Demand's artwork specification is stated as the lead knows it and is to be confirmed on the account before anything is uploaded.

1. What this is for

Shirts and hoodies that a stranger buys. The buyer does not know RiskMandate and does not need to. They buy it because the line is funny and true about risk, or because somebody they know holds the risk at work and will laugh, or because it is December. The mark is on it the way a brewery's mark is on a good beer shirt: present, small, and the reason a second person asks what it is.

So the order of work is: the joke first, the truth under the joke second, the mark third. A design that needs RiskMandate explained is a failed design for this brief.

2. What RiskMandate is, in one paragraph you may use

RiskMandate.ai helps organisations know what their AI agents can do. Its line is Know what your agents can do. Not what they did. What they can. Its product is an Agent Behaviour Policy (ABP): a short record, for one AI agent in one deployment, of everything the agent can reach, what the business authorised it to do, and the gap between the two. Around it sits a way of thinking about risk that the shirts borrow from: a risk exists the moment the exposure does; nobody can deny it, only accept it for a stated interval, fund the work that ends it, or fix it; silence is not a decision, it escalates to the boss; every risk has a holder and every path ends at the board; a rule in prose is a hope, and only a boundary the agent cannot reach is a control. RiskMandate is UK-based, early-stage and founder-led. It is not an insurer, and nothing it makes is a score.

4. The brand kit

Everything is on https://riskmandate.ai/brand.html, and the files are public.

  • The mark is a seal: a stamp with the monogram RM inside a ring, registration ticks on the axes, and a quiet inner ring. It signals a counter-signature. Files: https://riskmandate.ai/assets/brand/riskmandate-mark.svg (the containered mark, the default), riskmandate-mark-knockout.svg (no container, for dark garments), riskmandate-mark-small.svg (the simplified cut for anything under 24px, which on a shirt means a sleeve or a hem), riskmandate-mark-512.png, and the EPS cuts for a printer. The lockups with the wordmark are riskmandate-lockup-light.svg and riskmandate-lockup-dark.svg.
  • The colours are tokens: green #1A7F5A; the mark's container #161615; ink #0D0D0C; paper #F7F6F2; gold #B45309; red #C0392B; warm greys between. On a shirt, use green and ink on paper-coloured garments, and the knockout mark with paper and green on ink-coloured garments. Nothing else is on-brand, and the mark is never recoloured.
  • The type: the site uses the system sans and the system mono on purpose. For print, any clean grotesk (Inter, Helvetica, Archivo) for the line, and a mono (JetBrains Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Courier) for anything that should read as machine output: labels, counts, dates, the small print. The mono is where a lot of the humour lives.
  • Five things not to do with the mark: do not recolour it; do not use the detailed cut small; do not stretch, rotate or add effects; do not redraw the monogram; leave clear space of half its width on every side.
  • The voice: plain, dry, British spelling (behaviour, authorise, licence), no exclamation marks, no dash in a line. The joke is in the truth, not in the font.

5. The print rules

  • The usual Merch on Demand artwork: PNG, 4500 × 5400 pixels, transparent background, sRGB, under 25 MB, one file per design per colourway. Confirm on the account before uploading; Amazon changes it.
  • Two colourways of every design: one for dark garments (paper and green on transparent), one for light garments (ink and green on transparent). Never bake a garment colour into the file.
  • Keep the artwork inside the middle of the canvas with a margin; a front print is smaller than it looks on screen. Text smaller than about 6 mm high on the garment will not print cleanly, and lines thinner than about 2 px at full size will vanish. Big type, few words.
  • A hoodie front reads higher on the chest than a t-shirt; the same file works, but check the mock-up.
  • Legal, before anything goes on sale: no other company's name, logo or product name; no characters, memes or fonts owned by somebody else; no real person's face; nothing that reads as a certificate, a badge of compliance or a rating; and a trade-mark search on each line that will be sold. There is no deny button and Hope is not a control are ours to check first.

6. What a design must never carry

These are the site's rules, and they hold on cloth.

  1. No score, rating, level, traffic light or verdict. A count is fine (340 / 12); a grade is not.
  2. Never "ADP". The acronym is ABP, and on a shirt the words are better than the acronym anyway.
  3. Never "the policy" alone, because on this site policy is also the insurance instrument. The behaviour policy or the ABP.
  4. No conformity language: not certified, compliant, conformant, accredited, aligned.
  5. No named third party. No vendor, no insurer, no standard's text.
  6. **Never rung.** A ladder has steps here.
  7. No claim the shirt cannot carry: nothing about cover, nothing about being insurable.

9. The prompt to paste

Give the image model this brief's link, then the prompt below, one design at a time. Replace the parts in brackets.

You are designing a t-shirt and hoodie print for Amazon Merch on Demand. Read the brief at https://riskmandate.ai/admin/briefs/merch__funny-designs-for-people-who-hold-the-risk/index.md first, and follow its sections 4, 5 and 6 exactly.

Design: concept [number and name from section 8]. Line: "[the line, exactly as written in section 7]". [Figure: describe it in one sentence, or "type only".]

Style: plain and dry. A clean grotesk for the line, a mono face for any small print, counts or dates. Two colours plus the garment: green #1A7F5A and ink #0D0D0C for a light garment; green #1A7F5A and paper #F7F6F2 for a dark garment. No gradients, no drop shadows, no textures, no illustration of people. Big type, few words. The RiskMandate mark, small, below the design or at the hem, from https://riskmandate.ai/assets/brand/riskmandate-mark.svg (use the knockout cut on the dark version), never recoloured, never stretched, with clear space around it.

Output: (1) the artwork as a PNG, 4500 by 5400 pixels, transparent background, for the light garment; (2) the same for the dark garment; (3) a mock-up of each on a t-shirt and on a hoodie; (4) the line as typed, so it can be checked; (5) a listing title of at most sixty characters and three bullet points for the product page, in British spelling, with no claim about RiskMandate's product beyond what the brief's section 2 says; (6) the name of any font used.

Do not put a score, rating, level or traffic light anywhere. Do not use the words certified, compliant, conformant, accredited or aligned. Do not name any other company, product, insurer or standard. Do not write "ADP", and do not write "the policy" on its own. Do not use the word "rung".

10. What to send back, and what happens then

For each design: the two PNGs, the two mock-ups, the listing text, and the line. The lead chooses which go up. Before a line is listed, the trade-mark search in §5. The mark files are already public and need no permission; the taglines are RiskMandate's and may be used on RiskMandate's merchandise.

11. What this does not settle

  • Which lines sell. Amazon's tiering answers that, slowly; start with ten, not forty.
  • Prices, which are set on the account.
  • Whether the seal on its own (concept 9) is a shirt anybody outside the company buys. Probably not; it is the one design for insiders.
  • The second family the lead already sells, The Cyber Boardroom's: the same account, a different mark, and none of these lines.
