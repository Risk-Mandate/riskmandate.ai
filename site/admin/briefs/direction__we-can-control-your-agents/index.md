## 2. What it asks of riskmandate.ai

1. **One message, simple.** "We can control your agents", from one agent to hundreds. Less fluffy, less theoretical,
   "in your face": if you have agents, we can understand what they do and we can control them.
2. **Reality and proof first.** ABPs run in production on our own team, the data, the screenshots, the Web Summit
   agents. The old site "was trying to sell stuff that wasn't actually usable … there wasn't real world examples".
3. **Many small situations, and the big one.** The micro: "the conductors … were mapping this … the developers …
   needed permission for this … even the cases where it didn't work, and then we put a policy on it". The macro: a
   team of more than a hundred agents at the summit.
4. **An honest order of strength.** "Putting policies just for agents is not the best, but that gets you a lot of
   value for money right now." Then the advanced modes: SHarness, hooks. Open source, no lock-in, works with
   whatever is on the other side. "If you need help controlling your agents, we are your person."
5. **Visual and professional.** The film ("it's a great video"); many Web Summit photos; several designs of the same
   data, as on Silvio Switha's site; recordings of the animations; the universe view's white stripes at the border
   fixed.
6. **An archive, not a deletion.** Older content moved out of the way and kept ("interesting from evolution").
7. **One ladder, the same as agenticcells.ai**: £500 (an agentic flow we run ourselves, no meetings, low touch: here,
   a behaviour policy), £1,500 (preparation, two meetings, work, presentation, delivery), £5,000 (about two days,
   heavier lifting, maybe travel), £20,000 (a customer engagement).
8. **Both sites sell each other.** A policy alone is RiskMandate; an agentic team is AgentiCCells, usually with
   policies; a dual sale is possible. Link agenticcells.ai and the hub; they link us for control of agents.
9. **The book.** Offer the PDF and a link to buy: *One Human, Nine Agents, Nineteen Hours*,
   https://leanpub.com/one-human-nine-agents-nineteen-hours ("free to download, pay what you want", per
   newsroom.sgit.ai/articles/the-book-is-on-leanpub.html). "Linpa" in the dictation is Leanpub.
10. **The team.** Developer, designer, sherpa, synthetic users, ambassador, and researchers digging the case studies
    and taking screenshots, improving the site on a loop every hour.

## 3. Where it meets the site's rules

1. **Prices.** The ladder replaces £10 / £50 / £500 / £1,500. The words are the lead's, but they reached this
   session through another agent; the live pricing page changes when the lead confirms it directly. Until then the
   ladder is shown on the proposal pages, marked as proposed. Open: what becomes of the £10 pack and the £50 vault
   (kept as self-serve below the ladder, or retired), and the free early-user route.
2. **The rules hold.** No score on a behaviour policy (a situation shows a count, never a rating); British English;
   ABP, never the other acronym; never "the policy" alone; never test somebody else's system; no conformity words.
   "We can control your agents" is said beside what was not done: the summit ABPs were in observe mode.
3. **People and photos.** Only people who agreed are named; photos only from material already public (the stand
   site's photos sit in an encrypted vault with a published read key); AI-made images are marked; the excluded
   client site is never named or linked.
4. **Nobody is contacted.** Outreach is drafted; the lead sends.
