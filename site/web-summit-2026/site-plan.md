## Settled before anything is carried across.

The newsroom is the lead’s thinking out loud; the site is what we sell. Where they differ, the site’s rules win and the difference goes to the lead.

- **Prices.** The site: £10, £50, £500, £1,500, one-off. The newsroom also proposes prepaid credit (£5 or £50), services at £5,000 and £25,000, and one ladder for every site at £500 to £20,000. All are labelled proposals there. Nothing changes on the site without the lead.
- **The roadmap.** The site: ABP, Licence to Operate, Insurability Index. The newsroom’s roadmap of 20 October has six stages and calls itself “a proposal, not a decision”.
- **Words the site does not use.** Some articles use conformity words, a scale of levels for deciders, a traffic light in a collaborator’s app, or verdicts on named companies. Those passages are not carried; the map marks each one.
- **Claims as if shipped.** The ten questions of 9 October found the site describing continuous attestation and a first Index as if built. The _What runs_ page is where that is settled.
- **Invented examples.** Pellbridge, Northstar, Thistlewood, Tallyline and others are invented. Each carries the word at the top, wherever it appears.
- **Counts that move.** One article gives 2,032 run reports; the summit record, a day later, 2,262. A count is reprinted with its date or not at all.

## Read, not rewritten.

The agents that read the newsroom skimmed each article from its abstract, its short list and its “what this does not claim” section, and read in full where a figure or a caution needed it. A row on the map is a pointer, not a reprint; every page built from it rereads the article first. No page on the live site was changed; the menu was not changed; nothing was sent to anybody.
