The lead’s brief changes the plan: we can control your agents.

The same night, the lead dictated one brief for riskmandate.ai and agenticcells.ai together (recorded with his words). Where it differs from the shape below, it wins.

  • One message. “We can control your agents”, from one agent to hundreds; less theory, more proof. The homepage draft is rebuilt around it: home-proposal.
  • Situations, small and large. Real cases from our own agents (a permission needed, a gap found, a rule that failed and became a barrier) beside the hundred-agent picture. A situations page moves to the top of the backlog.
  • An honest order of strength. A policy first (“a lot of value for money right now”), then hooks, then a boundary with SHarness. Open source, no lock-in.
  • One ladder with agenticcells.ai. £500, £1,500, £5,000, £20,000, proposed; the live prices stay until the lead confirms. The two sites link each other: a policy alone is RiskMandate, a team is AgentiCCells.
  • An archive. Older pages move out of the menu and are kept. This replaces most of the rewrites in section 03: a page that does not serve the message goes to the archive rather than being rebuilt.
  • The film, the photos, the book. The film on the homepage; Web Summit photos of people who agreed; One Human, Nine Agents, Nineteen Hours with its Leanpub link.
  • A team on an hourly loop. Developer, designer, sherpa, synthetic users, ambassador, researchers, as sub-agents of one conductor; the backlog is task brief T18.
01 · What the reading found

The argument is written. The site does not carry it.

The newsroom has been writing RiskMandate in public since August, an article most days. The site carries sixteen example ABPs, four levels and a model; the newsroom carries the reasons, the measurements, the case of our own agents, the insurance argument and the replies to people who disagree. Five things stand out.

  • Where a rule is kept is the idea that recurs most. Prompt, hook or boundary; expectation, setting or boundary; “a rule the agent can rewrite is a setting”. Thirty-three of the items on the map feed it (Hope or enforcement, Where a check runs, Proven controls for agents: “22 were expectations, 34 settings and one a boundary”). The site has no page for it.
  • The insurance argument is a series, not a page. Five articles of 28 October and the whole-story piece rebuild insurance.html into a family (Insuring agents, the whole story, A payout for every row). They say themselves that no insurer has agreed to any of it.
  • Our own agents are the first case. The summit record, the ultimate insider, six agents, one inbox, the push to main. And one measured workflow on a system we are entitled to run: our workflow on Odoo 20, 25 probes.
  • There are vendor-shaped examples, read from documentation. Manus, Cloudflare and Lovable, each “from [the vendor]’s documentation, read on 9 November 2026”, never run. They belong beside the sixteen, marked as documented.
  • The two features the lead named are the reviewed level, explained. Local trust, certified upwards says the person closest to a story vouches for it and the vouching travels up; on this site that is the reviewer closest to the deployment signing the ABP for a buyer or an insurer. Sources and their shadows is our rules 4 and 8 written as a method: every reference with its access, its quote and its date, and disagreements kept unresolved.

04 · Where the newsroom and the site disagree

Settled before anything is carried across.

The newsroom is the lead’s thinking out loud; the site is what we sell. Where they differ, the site’s rules win and the difference goes to the lead.

  • Prices. The site: £10, £50, £500, £1,500, one-off. The newsroom also proposes prepaid credit (£5 or £50), services at £5,000 and £25,000, and one ladder for every site at £500 to £20,000. All are labelled proposals there. Nothing changes on the site without the lead.
  • The roadmap. The site: ABP, Licence to Operate, Insurability Index. The newsroom’s roadmap of 20 October has six stages and calls itself “a proposal, not a decision”.
  • Words the site does not use. Some articles use conformity words, a scale of levels for deciders, a traffic light in a collaborator’s app, or verdicts on named companies. Those passages are not carried; the map marks each one.
  • Claims as if shipped. The ten questions of 9 October found the site describing continuous attestation and a first Index as if built. The What runs page is where that is settled.
  • Invented examples. Pellbridge, Northstar, Thistlewood, Tallyline and others are invented. Each carries the word at the top, wherever it appears.
  • Counts that move. One article gives 2,032 run reports; the summit record, a day later, 2,262. A count is reprinted with its date or not at all.
05 · What this page did not do

Read, not rewritten.

The agents that read the newsroom skimmed each article from its abstract, its short list and its “what this does not claim” section, and read in full where a figure or a caution needed it. A row on the map is a pointer, not a reprint; every page built from it rereads the article first. No page on the live site was changed; the menu was not changed; nothing was sent to anybody.
