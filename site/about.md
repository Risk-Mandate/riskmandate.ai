## One question, one document, and the document is the product.

The question RiskMandate answers is narrow: _what can this agent we just connected actually do, and who said it could?_ The product is the Agent Behaviour Policy, written in the open as [sixteen template policies](agent-behaviour-policy.html) for the shapes people run, and sold at [four levels](pricing.html), from a pack to a reviewed policy with a named person’s signature. The site, the templates and the tooling are open source; riskmandate.ai is one of the [sgit.ai](https://sgit.ai/) network of sites, and every vault it publishes is encrypted with its read key printed on purpose.

### The Agent Behaviour Policy available now

For one agent in one deployment: the grant measured on the thing itself, the mandate in the deployer’s words, the gap derived, a barrier on every row. Never a score.

### The Licence to Operate template

The business is the authority, the policy is the instrument, the agent is the licensee. [The demo](licence-to-operate.html) shows a licence written against a behaviour policy, with a term and a condition.

### The Insurability Index in design

A dated record of the deployment, with the residual risk explicitly owned, in a form an insurer can read. The Index is where the deployment gets a number; the behaviour policy never does.

The company, for the record: RiskMandate’s early trading runs through [The Cyber Boardroom](https://www.thecyberboardroom.com/), a company incorporated in the UK and the lead’s earlier venture; a company for RiskMandate alone, between the two founders, is being set up. The line is said in full only on [the early-adopters page](early-adopters.html), where it matters, because the people that page thanks were The Cyber Boardroom’s early users.
