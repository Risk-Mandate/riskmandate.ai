What it is

The same document as every other level, with a person in the room.

Every level sells one Agent Behaviour Policy for one agent in one deployment: everything it can reach, what you authorised, the gap between them, and what actually stands in the way of each thing. What the top level adds is the part that cannot be automated — somebody asking your team what is really running, correcting the mandate in the room, and putting their name to the result. Session one: what is actually running

Half an hour. Not a questionnaire and not a form: the reviewer asks what the agent is connected to, who set it up, what has changed since, and what you believed you had authorised. The answer “nobody decided that” is a useful answer here and is written down as one.

The reach is assembled for your shape, each row labelled by how it is known — measured, documented or derived. The mandate is written from what your team said, in their words. What we ask of you, and what we never ask for

An hour, two people, and no access to anything.

What you bring

  • An hour, across two half-hour sessions, usually a few days apart.
  • Two people: whoever built or runs the agent, and whoever answers for what it touches. One of each is enough; more is fine.
  • A list of what it connects to — repositories, mailboxes, drives, ticketing, cloud accounts — at the level of names, not credentials.
  • The willingness to say what was actually authorised, including where the honest answer is that nobody decided.

What we never ask for

  • Credentials, tokens or keys. Not one, at any level.
  • Access to your systems. We do not connect to anything of yours, and nothing we deliver sits in your request path.
  • A scan, a test or a probe. We describe a deployment from what you tell us and what the vendors publish. We never test somebody else's system.
  • Your data. What the agent processes is none of our business; what it can reach is the whole of it.

What it is not

Four things this is not, said before you ask.

  • Not a score. A behaviour policy describes and carries no rating, level or verdict. The document says what the agent can reach and what stands in the way; whether that is acceptable is your decision and it belongs to you.
  • Not a compliance assessment, and not a certificate. Neither word appears on the deliverable, and no standards body has assessed anything here.
  • Not a penetration test. Nothing is probed, nothing is exploited, and nothing of yours is touched.
  • Not insurance. We are not an insurer and we place no cover. Where this is going is a separate argument on a separate page.
