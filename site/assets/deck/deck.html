What an Agent Behaviour Policy is

Four objects, and only one of them is an opinion.

Measured

Reach

Everything the agent can actually access in this deployment, including what nobody thought to check.

Elicited

Mandate

The job, written down: what the business authorised it to do. The one part only you can supply.

Derived

Gap

Reach minus mandate. Never written by hand, and recomputed whenever either side moves.

Recorded

Barriers

What actually stands in the way of each capability: controls, constraints and open questions.

A behaviour policy describes and carries no score. What is acceptable is a decision, and the decision belongs to the people who answer for the agent.

Consequences

What follows when a capability meets an asset: read, act, exfiltrate, disrupt, impersonate, outlive. Each one names what it requires and the articles it touches.

Who holds the barrier

A scope you consented to and can revoke is a different object from a product decision a vendor can change in a release. Both are recorded; the difference is in the facts, not an adjective.

Validity

Every policy says what it describes, as at which date, and what makes it void: a release, a setting, a connector enabled. If the risk changed, the deployment changed.

A licence to operate with a named owner and an expiry, and a maturity model for how acceptance travels up to the board. The policy carries no score; the acceptance carries a name.

Why this year

Three things changed, and none of them was us.

1 January 2026

Cover started being withdrawn

A generative-AI exclusion took effect in the standard liability forms much of one large insurance market runs on. Several carriers have filed their own, one of them absolute.

9 March 2026

Responsibility was assigned

A national consumer regulator said a business is responsible if an agent it uses does something illegal, and should be clear what the agent may do and what data it can access.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

Residual risk must be judged acceptable

For high-risk AI systems, Article 9(5) requires the residual risk of each hazard, and overall, to be judged to be acceptable. Somebody has to do the judging, and they need a record to judge from.

The first two are sourced and dated on riskmandate.ai/insurance.html. The third is quoted from the regulation as published, read 24 September 2026.
