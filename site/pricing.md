# The library is free. Your own is not.

What is for sale today is one document: a behaviour policy for the deployment you actually run, derived rather than authored, with every row's provenance attached. Everything we use to argue for it — the worked examples, the capability data, the draft for your shape — is free and needs no account. Below is exactly where that line falls, and what the paid side buys that the free side cannot.

## The library is the argument. The instance is yours.

If the free thing is not genuinely useful nobody arrives, and if it is too useful nobody pays. So the split is not by feature: it is that everything describing _a_ deployment is free, and the document describing _your_ deployment is not.

- **The free draft is the top of the sales motion rather than a giveaway.** Correcting our draft is how you tell us what you actually intended, and the intent is the one input we cannot derive. So the free tier and the elicitation are the same step, and that is by design.
- **The prompt is free on purpose, and it is not sold as a skill.** The portable part of the agent-skill format carries six fields, and the ones that would actually constrain an agent are not among them — so a skill distributed to somebody else can carry instructions and _cannot_ carry a constraint. That is a field list rather than an opinion, and it is why the document that bounds nothing is the free one.
- **And the format sets the price more than the content does.** The same argument sells for a few pounds bundled as a skill, around eighty as a document pack, and into four figures as a toolkit. Nothing about the content explains that spread; the container does. We would rather sell the vault, which is the only container that recomputes.

A behaviour policy reduces accidents. It does not stop an attacker. Here is what would.

A rule somebody wrote down is not enforced by anything the agent does not already control, so it shapes what an agent **tends** to do rather than what it is capable of doing — the model provider's own words, and we would rather quote them than talk around them. That is worth money when the failure mode is tendency, and most of what goes wrong is not an attack: it is an agent doing something reasonable that nobody wanted, because nobody told it. What bounds a grant is a setting, a scope or a gateway, and [Lab 05](lab-commit-author.html) works one case all the way through — including the four free settings you should turn on before paying us anything.

**And if your agent does something you did not authorise, the question you will be asked is what you _did_ authorise.** That document is the thing on the paid side of the line above.

A floor of ten pounds stands on card-fee arithmetic rather than on positioning, and no price above it is settled. We would rather charge one price and count than ask thirty people what they would pay — stated willingness to pay overstates the real thing by between one and three times, and naming a number before the question is what produces the answer to it.

## Value grows up the ladder. The security model does not change.

Separately from the document above, the platform a policy lives in is itself operated at three tiers. Each bullet is a maintainability, scalability, or security property — labelled per axis — not a feature behind a paywall.

- MAINTAINOpen source, files and folders, no database to operate — no lock-in.
- SCALEAdopt incrementally: as much or as little as fits your stack, at your pace.
- SECUREZero-knowledge vault on your own infrastructure — your keys, your data.
- MAINTAINManaged runtime — versioned, provenance on every change, agent-operable.
- SCALEGrows with usage; pre-approval against risk profiles keeps acceptance scaling without nagging.
- SECURESame zero-knowledge foundation — plaintext never leaves your endpoints.
- MAINTAINSLAs, support, and guided upgrades across the version chain.
- SCALEThe underwriting chain org-wide — acceptance propagated level by level to a board view.
- SECURESovereign deployment: your region, your keys; NIST / ISO / EU AI Act alignment.

Value grows up the ladder; the security model does not change. Sovereignty is the floor, not the premium — every tier is zero-knowledge on your own infrastructure.
